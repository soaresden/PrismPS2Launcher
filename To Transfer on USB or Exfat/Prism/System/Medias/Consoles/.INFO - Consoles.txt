Photographs of the machines
===========================

One picture per system, named after the folder Prism uses:

    nes.jpg   snes.jpg   megadrive.jpg   psx.jpg   ps2.jpg   ...

.jpg or .png, both work - Enceladus reads BMP, JPG and PNG. Around 420 px wide is
right: the panel draws it at 210, and anything larger is memory spent on nothing.

The systems view shows it beside the console's name, its processor, how many were
sold and what it was. When there is no photograph it falls back to the console's
logo from Medias/Logos/, and when there is not even that, to a plain plate in the
manufacturer's colour. Nothing breaks; the screen just says less.


Getting them
------------

    HelperScripts\GetConsolePhotos.ps1

It searches Wikimedia Commons and keeps ONLY images in the public domain, checking
the licence of each one from Commons' own metadata rather than assuming. Most of
the good console photographs there were taken by Evan-Amos, who released them into
the public domain - no attribution required, no share-alike clause to carry. It
writes SOURCES.txt beside the pictures with the Commons page, the author and the
licence of every file it kept, and lists what it refused and why.


Using your own instead
----------------------

Drop any picture in, named after the folder. A photograph you took, a render, a
screenshot of the box - it is your install.

What must NOT go here is artwork from a theme you downloaded. The PlayStation-X
theme has a beautiful console picture for every system, and it is CC BY-NC-SA:
free to use, not free to redistribute inside a project anyone can clone, and the
non-commercial clause is incompatible with the GPL. Copy one onto your own stick
if you like it; do not commit it.
